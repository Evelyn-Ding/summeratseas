## Overview

<!-- 2-3 sentences describing the project goals -->
Vocal biomarkers are a powerful yet underexplored approach for detecting a wide range of health conditions. From mental health disorders to laryngeal cancer, many conditions cause changes to a patient’s voice that can be captured by acoustic features. A growing field of research involves training models to understand these complex features and make diagnoses. However, existing studies often rely on models that are limited by their lack of interpretability. This project aims to develop automated diagnostic models that are highly interpretable, providing clinically relevant outputs rather than black-box predictions. First, datasets focused on raw speech audio from patients with various medical conditions were accessed from platforms like Zenodo and PhysioNet. Next, established acoustic feature sets (e.g., eGeMAPS) were extracted to construct interpretable baseline classifiers and to examine which features are most predictive across different conditions. Building upon these baselines, the study explored more advanced speech processing approaches, using self-supervised speech models and recent audio-language models such as Qwen-Audio and Flamingo-Audio. These models were utilized in multiple ways, including the extraction of interpretable speech representations as well as the development of end-to-end systems for automated health assessment. Finally, post hoc explanation techniques, such as SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP), were applied to analyze model behavior and to enhance the interpretability of predictions.
